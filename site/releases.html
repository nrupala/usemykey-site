Releases

Every release of MyKey, what changed in it, and how it was tested. The testing record is part of the release — a release without one is not finished. Thank you to everyone who runs MyKey and tells us what breaks.

2026-10-24 — Licence recovery and returns, in plain words

What changed:

  • Licence recovery is now email-only: email us from the address you paid with and the key is re-sent to that same address. We no longer ask for a date of birth or a payment date — we hold the purchase details on our side, and control of your inbox is the proof the request is yours.
  • Returns are stated plainly on the Buy page: an unredeemed licence may be returned for store credit within 30 days of purchase; after 30 days a licence is treated as redeemed and the sale is final. Duplicate charges, a key we failed to deliver and cannot recover, and anything the law requires are always refunded. MyKey never phones home, so we cannot see whether a key was used or switch one off — the policy says so, and says why.

Testing record: after publishing, the Recover, Buy, and Terms pages were fetched and checked live — the new recovery steps and return wording serving, no date-of-birth request anywhere on the site, and the checkout configuration byte-identical to before (billing path untouched).

2026-10-19 — Billing, hardened: one payment, one licence

What changed:

  • The order recorder behind the Stripe webhook is now write-once: a re-delivered payment event can never move an order backwards or mint a second licence for one payment. The issuer also dedupes by checkout session, so the same payment cannot be issued twice even if a run is interrupted halfway.
  • Chargebacks are handled end to end: a dispute flags the licence, a lost dispute revokes it as a chargeback, and a won dispute clears the flag. Refunds and disputes belonging to other products on the shared Stripe account are ignored — they write no MyKey records.

Testing record: desktop suite after the fix: 282 passed, 1 skipped, including 6 new issuer tests. A duplicate-delivery re-proof ran against the real deployed site and order store: the same verified payment event delivered twice left the order byte-identical and produced exactly one licence. Release gate (billing fittings): PASS WITH FINDINGS, with both findings — the idempotency gap and the dispute gap — recorded fixed.

Release gate: GRADUATED — RELEASE, 2026-10-24 (early graduation on completed evidence; register row 22).

2026-10-19 — Checkout opens

What changed:

  • Licence checkout is live on Stripe: pay what you want (US$49 preset, US$29 minimum) or a fixed US$29, plus the optional patron tips of US$5 or US$3 a month.
  • After payment, Stripe notifies this site, the order is recorded, and the licence key is minted under the licence authority and emailed to the buyer automatically. A refund marks the licence revoked in the registry.

Testing record: the purchase cycle was exercised end to end in Stripe's test mode — payment, order recorded, key minted and emailed, refund, licence revoked — before the live switch. Desktop suite re-run on file: 276 passed, 1 skipped. The live Stripe products, prices and payment links were read back from Stripe and confirmed live. Release gate (billing fittings): PASS WITH FINDINGS.

2026-10-14 — Android 1.4.0: your vault has a home

What changed:

  • Give your vault a home: pick a folder once — for example in your cloud drive — and MyKey keeps the encrypted vault file there, updated on every save. Uninstalling the app no longer takes your vault with it, and a reinstall offers to open the vault found in its folder. If the copies ever disagree, you are asked at unlock; nothing is overwritten on a guess.
  • Permanent release signing: the Android app is now signed with the company's permanent release key, so updates install in place. (Builds from the throwaway-signed test days need one final fresh install.)
  • The new MyKey mark — the refined key — in every icon and as this site's favicon.
  • “Help and questions” moved up on the locked screen, above the fold, where a locked-out user can see it.

Testing record: this is the client measured by the product's first release gate (2026-10-15), run in clean environments: the phone/web client suite ran 194 tests, all passed (16 files), and its production build was clean. The served APK's SHA-256 matched the published value, its signing certificate is the company's permanent release key, and the app requests no INTERNET permission. Gate verdict for the product: PASS WITH FINDINGS — no Critical, no High.

2026-10-13 — Desktop 2.2.0 · Android 1.3.0: the whole vault

What changed:

  • Vault v3: every entry is encrypted under its own key. An authorized agent gets a scoped key that cannot decrypt anything outside its scope — enforced by the encryption itself, not by a promise in the code.
  • Sync through your own cloud folder, and two-factor backup packages (your recovery phrase plus a one-time backup code, together). Devices, folders and backup targets are paired by fingerprint, inside the encrypted vault.
  • The licence: free up to 50 passwords and 4 connections, with every safety feature included; a signed one-time key unlocks unlimited passwords and 20 connections.
  • Passkeys kept in the vault, password age and rotation dates, built-in help and FAQ, the browser extension, and Android autofill.
  • Desktop downloads published: the Windows app and CLI and the Linux bundle, with a SHA-256 checksums file. The builds are unsigned — the Download page says so, in plain words.

Testing record: from the projects' own changelogs: the agent-access round ran 89 tests in a fresh environment; the full desktop suite stood at 241 passed, 1 skipped after the packaging fixes; the Windows build was built and smoke-tested on a real Windows 11 machine; the phone client's backup packages were cross-checked — opened by the desktop engine — in both directions. The same software was then measured end to end by the 2026-10-15 release gate: desktop suite 276 passed, 1 skipped in a fresh environment, the shipped 2.2.0 code itself 209 passed, and a 9-of-9 tamper battery plus a licence-forgery battery were rejected on every attempt.
